## 1. Which products and tools check whether paper X supports claim Y? What does each do well and badly?

### Takeaway
No mainstream commercial product checks whether a cited paper supports the specific sentence that cites it. scite, Elicit, Consensus, SciSpace and Semantic Scholar each provide useful parts: how other papers cite a work, retraction flags, full-text question answering, and citation contexts. The tools that do the actual job are open-source or research systems from 2025 and 2026: SemanticCite, sciwrite-lint, RefVerifier and the PaperQA2 evidence engine. The best agreement reported on real manuscripts is about 71% (RefVerifier), so verdicts still need quoted evidence and human review.

#### scite (Smart Citations, Reference Check, Assistant, MCP)
- What and method: scite shows the text around each citation in citing papers and a deep-learning label saying whether the statement supports, contrasts with, or merely mentions the cited work (Nicholson et al., Quantitative Science Studies 2021, DOI 10.1162/qss_a_00146) — [QSS 2021 via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/81fa50fb3c294f6ba01cc597d41a974e).
- Reference Check: the user uploads a manuscript PDF. The report shows how the manuscript cites each reference, flags retractions and other editorial notices, and shows how often each reference has been supported, contrasted or mentioned by later papers. It is a premium feature with a free trial for new accounts — [scite help](https://help.scite.ai/zh/article/how-do-i-use-the-scite-reference-check-u52q0w); [CUHK LibGuide](https://libguides.lib.cuhk.edu.hk/scite/references); [Aaron Tay on retraction checking](https://aarontay.substack.com/p/checking-for-retractions-other-quality).
- Accuracy reported by the developers: a library guide quotes developer figures of 97% for mentioning, 64% for supporting and 59% for contrasting — [HKUST Library](https://library.hkust.edu.hk/sc/trial-scite/). In an interview, the CEO said contrasting is the hardest class and mentioning the easiest — [Aaron Tay interview](https://aarontay.substack.com/p/scite-super-power-for-researchers).
- Independent accuracy: Bakker, Theis-Mahon and Brown (Hypothesis, 2023) took 324 citations from systematic reviews that cited retracted papers. scite had classified only 98 of them. scite labeled 2 as supporting and 96 as mentioning; human coders labeled 42 supporting, 39 mentioning and 17 contrasting. F-measures ranged from 0.0 to 0.58 — [Hypothesis 2023](https://journals.indianapolis.iu.edu/index.php/hypothesis/article/view/26528). scite's reply argues that citations to retracted works in systematic reviews are an unrepresentative sample — [Hypothesis reply 2024](https://journals.indianapolis.iu.edu/index.php/hypothesis/article/view/28018).
- Price (third-party, conflicting): about $20/month, or $12/month billed annually, with a 7-day trial and no permanent free tier as of Q3 2026 — [AI Agent Index](https://theaiagentindex.com/agents/scite-ai). CostBench also lists $20 per user per month — [CostBench](https://www.costbench.com/compare/elicit-vs-scite/). Toolradar lists "Contact us" — [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/scite/pricing). scite's own pricing page (dated 2026-08-25) says a free account receives 25 MCP credits per month — [scite pricing.md](https://scite.ai/pricing.md).
- Data handling (third-party claim): uploaded PDFs are said to be stored in a private S3 bucket only while the report is generated — [Manusights comparison](https://manusights.com/blog/manusights-vs-scite).

#### PaperQA2 (FutureHouse)
- Method: agentic retrieval over full-text PDFs. It (1) searches for papers and chunks, embeds and indexes them; (2) gathers evidence by ranking the top-k chunks, having an LLM write a scored summary of each chunk for the query, and re-scoring the summaries ("re-ranking and contextual summarization", RCS); (3) writes the answer from the best summaries — [GitHub README](https://github.com/Future-House/paper-qa).
- Metadata: retrieved from Semantic Scholar, Crossref and Unpaywall, with fixes for OpenAlex; includes "citation counts with a retraction check" — [GitHub README](https://github.com/Future-House/paper-qa).
- Contradiction setting: a bundled `contracrow` setting takes a claim as the query and flags whether it is contradicted — [GitHub README](https://github.com/Future-House/paper-qa).
- December 2025 release: added tables, figures, equations and non-English text; added Docling and Nvidia nemotron-parse PDF readers; all readers report page numbers; versioning switched to CalVer (for example v2025.12.17) — [GitHub README](https://github.com/Future-House/paper-qa).
- Accuracy: on LitQA2 (248 multiple-choice questions designed to need the main text rather than the abstract), PaperQA2 reached 85.2% precision and 66.0% accuracy, and answered "insufficient information" about 21.9% of the time. ContraCrow, built on PaperQA2, found 2.34 ± 1.99 contradictions per biology paper, and human experts validated 70% of them — [arXiv 2409.13740](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13740v2); [FutureHouse](https://www.futurehouse.org/paperqa). These are the authors' own preprint results (September 2024).
- License: Apache 2.0 — [GitHub LICENSE](https://github.com/Future-House/paper-qa).
- Criticism: a July 2026 preprint argues that the citation checks used by agentic systems such as OpenScholar and PaperQA2 are unreliable. On identical agent outputs, the unsupported-citation rate ranged from about 3% to about 18% depending only on how strict the verifier was — [chatpaper summary](https://chatpaper.com/paper/313100); [arXiv 2607.20527](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20527). The match between that summary and the arXiv paper was not fully verified.

#### Elicit
- Vendor-reported accuracy: extraction accuracy of 94–99%. An internal method compared outputs with 128 manually verified gold answers and gave 94%. LLM-based checking of extractions agreed with manual checking 89% of the time — [Elicit blog](https://elicit.com/blog/how-we-evaluated-elicit-systematic-review).
- Independent evaluation (Cambridge Core, seven life and environmental science reviews): 70 of 90 prompts exceeded an 87% accuracy threshold, but performance dropped on new articles. Repeated runs from different accounts agreed on extracted values 90% of the time, but on the supporting quotes only 46% and on the reasoning 30%. In "high-accuracy mode" these figures were 77%, 10% and 0% — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/C97DAEC70C3173A260F0B12E729E7250/core-reader).
- Price (third-party, conflicting): Pro $49/month billed annually and Scale $169/month (checked June 2026) — [aiproductivity.ai](https://aiproductivity.ai/pricing/elicit/). The top plan reportedly rose from $79 to $169 per user per month on 2026-06-16 — [CostBench changelog](https://costbench.com/changelog/elicit-price-increase-2026-06/). One plan was reportedly removed in September 2026 — [CostBench changelog](https://costbench.com/changelog/elicit-research-plan-removed-2026-09/).

#### Consensus
- Method: for yes/no questions, language models sort the top results into yes, no or possibly. Only the first 20 results are used, and papers judged irrelevant or unclear are dropped. The answers shown are quotes taken from the papers, which the company says reduces hallucination — [Consensus Meter: guardrails](https://consensus.app/home/blog/consensus-meter/); [Introducing the Consensus Meter](https://consensus.app/home/blog/introducing-the-consensus-meter/).
- Admitted weakness: the classifier sometimes misclassifies and is "far from perfect" — [Consensus blog](https://consensus.app/home/blog/consensus-meter/). The meter counts papers and does not weigh study size, strength or recency — [third-party review](https://comparebestai.com/articles/consensus-ai-review).
- Pricing (third-party): the free plan caps AI features such as the meter; Premium removes the caps — [CASRAI guide](https://casrai.org/guides/consensus-ai-search-engine-guide).

#### SciSpace
- Its "Reference Checker" agent validates DOIs, checks citation style, fixes citation errors and exports bibliographies. The page does not say it checks whether a cited paper supports a claim — [SciSpace agent page](https://stagecf.scispace.com/agents/reference-checker-xkr8e2ko). SciSpace presents itself as a workspace for finding, reading and writing from the literature — [SciSpace vs scite](https://scispace.com/compare/scite).

#### Semantic Scholar (citation contexts, intents, snippet search, openAccessPdf)
- The references and citations endpoints accept the fields `contexts` (citing sentences), `intents` and `isInfluential` — [S2 reference-details notebook](https://media.sciencemediacenter.de/share/mewikoco-ds/12_s2_reference_details.html). The intent labels follow the SciCite scheme: Method, Background, Result — [TFDS SciCite](https://www.tensorflow.org/datasets/catalog/scicite).
- A snippet-search endpoint returns passages from paper text that match a query. Third-party wrappers list the fields text, snippetKind, section, snippetOffset and annotations, filters for year and venue, and up to 1,000 results — [ToolUniverse docs](https://zitniklab.hms.harvard.edu/ToolUniverse/tools/semantic_scholar_tools.html); [MCP wrapper](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/fegizii/SemanticScholarMCP/tools/search_snippets).
- `openAccessPdf` returns {url, status, license} — [Apify scraper docs](https://apify.com/crawlerbros/semanticscholar-scraper). The API key goes in the `x-api-key` header — [OpenAPI spec gist](https://gist.github.com/alexandreteles/c8bc00830e97eefa961e26c49aa666e7). The S2 community repository has not been maintained since 2025-01-23 — [allenai/s2-folks](https://github.com/allenai/s2-folks).

#### Citation-intent classifiers (SciCite, ACL-ARC and successors)
- SciCite (Cohan et al., NAACL 2019) introduced "structural scaffolds" and reported 67.9% F1 on the ACL-ARC benchmark — [N19-1361 PDF](https://preview.aclanthology.org/fix_video/N19-1361.pdf). An aggregator lists it at about 85 macro-F1 on SciCite — [hyper.ai](https://vercel.hyper.ai/en/papers/1904.01608).
- CitePrompt: 86.33 macro-F1 on SciCite and 68.39 on ACL-ARC (aggregator figures) — [hyper.ai](https://vercel.hyper.ai/en/papers/2304.12730). CiteFusion (2025, the authors' own claim): 89.60 on SciCite and 76.24 on ACL-ARC — [mirror of arXiv 2407.13329](https://fugumt.com/fugumt/paper_check/2407.13329v4_enmode). The figures come from different setups and are not directly comparable.

#### SemanticCite (University of Sydney, arXiv November 2025)
- What: checks a citation against the full text of the source, not only its abstract. Four classes: Supported; Partially Supported (the core claim is present, but qualifications or context are missing); Unsupported (the source lacks the claim, contradicts it, or misrepresents it); Uncertain (the evidence is too ambiguous) — [arXiv 2511.16198](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.16198); [review summary](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/semanticcite-citation-verification-with-ai-powered-full-text-analysis-and-evidence-based-reasoning).
- Method: split the reference into chunks and embed them; extract the core claim from the citing text; retrieve with BM25 plus dense vectors; rerank with FlashRank; classify with an LLM that returns reasoning, a confidence score and evidence snippets. It can run on fine-tuned Qwen3 models: a 1.7B "Refiner" that extracts claims and a 4B "Checker", or on any LiteLLM provider, Claude included — [GitHub README](https://github.com/sebhaan/semanticcite).
- Data: a released dataset of more than 1,000 citations across eight disciplines — [arXiv 2511.16198](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.16198).
- Accuracy: the authors report that the fine-tuned small models perform comparably to large commercial systems ("comparable to GPT-4 with 100x fewer resources") — [GitHub README](https://github.com/sebhaan/semanticcite). Per-class numbers were not retrieved.
- Speed: 5–15 seconds per citation with cloud models, 10–30 seconds with local models — [GitHub README](https://github.com/sebhaan/semanticcite).
- License: the README badge says MIT; enterprise licensing is also offered — [GitHub README](https://github.com/sebhaan/semanticcite).

#### sciwrite-lint (Samsonau, arXiv April 2026)
- An open-source manuscript linter that runs on the researcher's machine (installed from PyPI, using free public databases, one consumer GPU and open-weights models; manuscripts are not sent out). It checks that references exist, are not retracted and have metadata matching canonical records. It also downloads and parses cited papers to test whether they support the claims made about them, and follows their bibliographies one level further. It combines these into a per-reference reliability score — [arXiv 2604.08501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08501); [Emergent Mind](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2604.08501).
- Evaluation: 98.5% recall on injected errors, plus a false-positive analysis on 30 unseen papers adjudicated by an LLM — [arXiv 2604.08501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08501).

#### RefVerifier (arXiv September 2026)
- A semi-automated tool limited to cited works. It extracts citations, retrieves open-access copies of cited papers, locates evidence in the full text and predicts a verdict. It reports 71% verdict agreement on real manuscripts with a median runtime of 11 minutes. The authors say wider deployment depends on institutional partnerships for rights-cleared full text — [arXiv 2609.07652](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07652).

#### Other recent systems (2026)
- CiteAudit (arXiv February 2026): a benchmark built from hallucinated citations reported on OpenReview plus controlled fake references, with a taxonomy of fake-citation types. It uses a multi-agent verifier with metadata extraction, memory lookup, web retrieval, scholar search and a final judgment step. The authors report beating LLM and commercial baselines; numbers not verified — [arXiv 2602.23452](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.23452).
- CiteCheck (arXiv May 2026): detects LLM citation hallucinations in scientific text by retrieving candidate papers and using a structured LLM verifier with the labels Exact, Minor and Major. It reports 88.7 macro-F1 on a benchmark of 982 physics citations — [arXiv 2605.27700](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27700). An unrelated "CiteCheck" (February 2025) is a Chinese dataset for citation faithfulness in RAG — [arXiv 2502.10881](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.10881); [summary](https://sotaverified.org/papers/citecheck-towards-accurate-citation).
- ReCite (arXiv 2026-09-08): states that retrieval-based systems have mostly stopped fabricating papers but still misattribute, citing real papers that do not support the claim. It proposes an agent with citation-location perception, intent-aware query planning and reflective verification over real academic databases, trained on trajectories built from LaTeX sources. It reports better strict citation accuracy than larger models and general agents — [arXiv 2609.09156](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09156); [papers.cool](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2609.09156).
- PAU (Paper Analysis Utility, AIES): automated bibliographic verification that leaves the final judgment to human reviewers — [AAAI/AIES](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIES/article/view/43316).
- Manusights (commercial): checks citations against Crossref, PubMed, OpenAlex and arXiv and flags retractions and broken DOIs, which is existence checking rather than support checking — [Manusights](https://manusights.com/blog/manusights-vs-scite).

#### OpenScholar (UW and Ai2; Nature, 2026-02-04)
- A retrieval-augmented model that searches the literature and writes answers grounded in the sources. Its datastore is built from peS2o open-access papers, with extra retrieval from the Semantic Scholar API and web search, a trained reranker, an optional self-feedback loop and optional post-hoc citation attribution. The code is Apache 2.0 — [GitHub README](https://github.com/AkariAsai/OpenScholar).
- In its experiments, GPT-4o fabricated 78–90% of its research citations. OpenScholar cited as accurately as human experts. OpenScholar-8B beat GPT-4o by 6.1% and PaperQA2 by 5.5% in correctness on a ScholarQABench multi-paper synthesis task. Experts preferred OpenScholar-8B and OpenScholar-GPT-4o answers over expert-written answers 51% and 70% of the time, against 32% for GPT-4o — [UW News](https://www.washington.edu/news/2026/02/04/in-a-study-ai-model-openscholar-synthesizes-scientific-research-and-cites-sources-as-accurately-as-human-experts/); [Nature Asia press release](https://www.natureasia.com/en/info/press-releases/detail/9228). One commentary notes the comparisons use models that are now outdated — [Rogue Scholar commentary](https://rogue-scholar.org/records/yd37z-emb56).

### Inferences
- **scite**: Reference Check grades the standing of each reference (retractions, how others receive it). It does not test whether the draft's sentence matches what the reference says, so it cannot catch the motivating case ("prior work studied a" citing a paper that never mentions a). It is still useful as a CIT-05 signal and as a hint: if other papers cite the work for X and the draft cites it for Y, that is worth a look. The free MCP credits may give a cheap LOOKUP channel for citation statements. Its supporting and contrasting labels are not reliable enough to use as evidence on their own.
- **PaperQA2**: the parts most worth reusing are the "gather evidence" step (relevance-scored summaries of chunks, then re-scoring), the explicit abstention ("insufficient information"), page-numbered parsing, and the metadata and open-access fetching. Its Apache license allows this. It is built to answer questions over a corpus, so to verify a citation it must be restricted to the single cited document.
- **Elicit, Consensus, SciSpace**: no API for per-citation verification was found, and Elicit's supporting quotes were reproducible in only 46% of repeated runs. For our skill, every quote must be checked by a script against the source text, and lookups must be cached, as DESIGN.md already plans.
- **Semantic Scholar**: citation contexts show how other authors describe the cited paper, which is a free source of paraphrases and of the paper's "reputation". Snippet search can find passages when no PDF is available. Intents (background, method, result) can decide which citations need a deep check.
- **SemanticCite** is the closest open-source design to CIT-06. Its four labels, hybrid retrieval with reranking, and claim-refinement step can be copied directly, and it can call Claude through LiteLLM. **sciwrite-lint** and **RefVerifier** are the closest overall analogs to the whole skill. RefVerifier's 71% agreement is a realistic ceiling to expect at first.
- **OpenScholar**'s 78–90% fabrication rate for GPT-4o explains how an AI-assisted literature review ends up with bad citations. ReCite's observation that systems now cite real but non-supporting papers matches the coauthor's case exactly: the reference exists (so CIT-02 passes), yet CIT-06 fails.

## 2. What does the research say?

### Takeaway
Citation errors are common even in human-written work. Meta-analyses find that 17–25% of quotations in medicine are wrong and 8–12% are seriously wrong, with no improvement over time. Automatic detection remains hard: the best fine-tuned system on a biomedical citation-integrity corpus reached 0.52 macro-F1, and human annotators agreed only weakly on accuracy judgments (κ 0.31). LLM checkers help but swing between being too lenient (accepting citations that merely share a topic) and too strict (rejecting correct citations). The reusable ideas are: split claims into atomic parts, include a "partial" label, retrieve evidence before judging, and calibrate the verifier against a gold set.

### Cited Findings

#### 2a. Scientific claim verification datasets and models
- **SciFact** (Wadden et al., 2020, "Fact or Fiction: Verifying Scientific Claims"): claims were written by rewriting citation sentences ("citances") into atomic claims, then checked against the abstracts of the cited papers. Rationale-level labels are SUPPORT or CONTRADICT; the pipeline reports SUPPORTS, REFUTES or no evidence. Each claim records `cited_doc_ids`, and annotators often found evidence in only some of the cited abstracts (in the documented example, two of three) — [SciFact data docs](https://github.com/allenai/scifact/blob/master/doc/data.md); [SciFact README](https://github.com/allenai/scifact). The citance-to-claim data is released for training claim-generation models — [SciFact README](https://github.com/allenai/scifact).
- **SciFact-Open** (Findings of EMNLP 2022): about 500K abstracts. Systems built on the small SciFact corpus lose at least 15 F1 points in this open setting. Evidence was annotated by pooling the top predictions of four models — [arXiv 2210.13777](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.13777); [GitHub](https://github.com/dwadden/scifact-open).
- **MultiVerS** (Findings of NAACL 2022): uses weak supervision and the full abstract as context, trained on SciFact, CovidFact and HealthVer, and was state of the art on SciFact when released. MIT license; described as a research prototype — [GitHub README](https://github.com/dwadden/multivers).
- **Citation-integrity corpus** (Sarol, Ming, Radhakrishna, Schneider, Kilicoglu; Bioinformatics, June 2024): 100 highly cited biomedical papers and the 3,063 citations to them, annotated with the citing context, the evidence sentences in the cited article, and an accuracy label (accurate, not accurate, irrelevant). 39.18% of the citations had accuracy errors. The best system, a sentence retriever (BM25 with MonoT5 reranking) plus a fine-tuned claim-verification model, reached 0.59 micro-F1 and 0.52 macro-F1. GPT-4 with in-context examples reached 0.65 micro-F1 but 0.45 macro-F1: better on accurate citations, worse on erroneous ones — [PMC11231046](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11231046/); [Illinois Experts](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/assessing-citation-integrity-in-biomedical-publications-corpus-an/).
- **Follow-up by the same group** (Peer Review Congress 2025): tested Gemini 1.5 Pro, GPT-4o and LLaMA-3.1 (8B and 70B), comparing a single prompt with a three-step pipeline that mimics a human reviewer: find the citation context, retrieve the most relevant reference sentences, then judge accuracy. Inter-rater agreement was high for locating the citation context (Cohen's κ 0.96) but low for the accuracy judgment (κ 0.31) — [PRC abstract](https://peerreviewcongress.org/abstract/leveraging-large-language-models-for-detecting-citation-quotation-errors-in-biomedical-literature/); [PRC page](https://peerreviewcongress.org/?p=3229).
- **WiCE** (EMNLP 2023): Wikipedia sentences paired with the web pages they cite. Labels: supported, partially_supported, not_supported. It annotates which evidence sentences support the claim and which tokens in the claim are not supported. It includes "Claim-Split" prompts for breaking claims into sub-claims and notes that long cited documents need an evidence-retrieval step. Annotations are ODC-BY; code is MIT — [GitHub README](https://github.com/ryokamoi/wice); [arXiv 2303.01432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01432).
- **SIDE** (Meta, Petroni et al., "Improving Wikipedia Verifiability with AI"): a system for improving the citations on Wikipedia, with a public "verifier" demo at verifier.sideeditor.com. MIT license — [GitHub](https://github.com/facebookresearch/side); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=qfTqRtkDbWZ). Its method and results were not re-verified in this session (see Gaps).

#### 2b. How accurate are citations in published papers?
- **Jergas & Baethge, PeerJ 2015**: 28 studies (from 559 screened). Pooled quotation error rates: major 11.9% (95% CI 8.4–16.6), minor 11.5% (8.3–15.7), total 25.4% (19.5–32.4). Even the lowest study estimate of total errors was 6.7%; no sign of publication bias — [PeerJ](https://peerj.com/articles/1364).
- **Baethge & Jergas, 2025 update**: 46 studies and about 32,000 quotations. 16.9% of quotations were incorrect, about half of them major errors (8.0%). Heterogeneity was high, and meta-regression showed no significant improvement in recent years — [PMC12285159](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12285159/); [PRC abstract](https://peerreviewcongress.org/abstract/quotation-inaccuracy-in-medicine-a-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis/); [authorship per Jodi Schneider's blog](https://jodischneider.com/blog/?p=2916).
- **Error definitions (Mogull, PLoS ONE 2017)**: major errors are cited claims that the source fails to support, is unrelated to, or contradicts. Minor errors are oversimplifications, overgeneralizations or trivial inaccuracies — [Texas State repository](https://digital.library.txstate.edu/handle/10877/8673). Definitions differ across studies, which makes comparison hard — [PMC3167934](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3167934/).
- **Smith & Cumberledge, Proc. R. Soc. A 2020**: 250 random citations in high-impact general science journals; total quotation error rate 25% — [AHU repository](https://repository.ahu.edu/handle/20.500.12521/252). Press coverage gives different figures (8.5% "unsubstantiated" and 3.6% "partially unsubstantiated", from citations drawn from 500 articles with 26,344 references); these could not be reconciled with the 25% figure — [Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/quarter-citations-top-journals-wrong-or-misleading).
- **Management and marketing example (Wright & Armstrong, Interfaces 2008)**: an audit of studies citing Armstrong & Overton (1977) on nonresponse bias. In summaries of the article, 49 of the 50 citing studies examined reported its findings improperly, misuse of significance tests led 76% of the sample to conclude there was no nonresponse bias, and only one study adjusted for it. The authors suggest citing authors had not read the original or not understood it — [Penn repository](https://repository.upenn.edu/marketing_papers/170/); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/orinte/v38y2008i2p125-139.html).
- **Copying citations (Simkin & Roychowdhury)**: a model of how citation misprints spread (4,300 citations of one paper, 196 with misprints, 45 distinct) estimates that only about 20% of citers read the original — [arXiv cond-mat/0212043](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0212043); [Complex Systems 14 (2003)](https://www.complex-systems.com/abstracts/v14_i03_a05/). A later paper estimates that 70–90% of citations are copied from other reference lists — [arXiv 1109.2272](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/1109.2272).

#### 2c. LLM-based citation verification studies
- **Zhang & Abernethy** (University of Washington; arXiv November 2024; AMIA 2025): tested GPT-family models on an expert-annotated, general-domain set of statement–reference pairs from journal articles, varying how much reference text was supplied through retrieval. Conclusion: the models can flag erroneous citations with limited context and without fine-tuning. No headline numbers were retrieved — [arXiv 2411.06101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06101); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41726520/).
- **Huang, Zhang, Huang & Zhang** (arXiv June 2026; The Electronic Library 44(4)): fine-tuning improved quotation-error detection. Of three ways to add information from the cited work, using the cited work's abstract performed best. TokenSHAP was used for interpretability — [arXiv 2606.08589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08589); [Emerald](https://www.emerald.com/el/article-abstract/44/4/876/1379886/Detection-and-interpretability-analysis-of?redirectedFrom=fulltext).
- **SourceCheckup** (Wu, Wu, … Zou; Nature Communications 16:3615, April 2025): an automated pipeline that checks whether each statement in an LLM's medical answer is supported by the source the model cited. Across seven LLMs, 800 questions and 58,000 statement–source pairs, 50–90% of responses were not fully supported, and some were contradicted by their sources. Even GPT-4o with web search left about 30% of individual statements unsupported, and nearly half of its responses were not fully supported. Independent assessments by doctors agreed with the automated findings. The 2024 preprint reported that GPT-4 agreed with a panel of doctors 88% of the time on source relevance — [Stanford Law page](https://law.stanford.edu/publications/an-automated-framework-for-assessing-how-well-llms-cite-relevant-medical-references/); [arXiv 2402.02008](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.02008); [PMC12003634](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12003634/).
- **CiteME** (Press et al., NeurIPS 2024 Datasets and Benchmarks): the reverse task, finding the paper an excerpt cites. Humans scored 69.7% (38.2 seconds on average). Frontier LMs without tools scored 4.2–18.5%, and SPECTER2 retrieval 0%. CiteAgent (GPT-4o with search and reading) scored 35.3%. Code is MIT; data CC-BY-4.0 — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/0ef47f7b768e1a012e3d995ac8d8fac7-Abstract.html); [GitHub](https://github.com/bethgelab/CiteME).
- **CiteGuard** (ACL 2026): retrieval-augmented validation reaches up to 68.1% on CiteME, against a human score of 69.2% (69.7% in v3). The authors report that LLM-as-a-judge alone often rejects correct citations, with recall as low as 16–17% in a ChatGPT-4o test — [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.acl-long.282/); [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17853v3).
- **"Is this Citation on Point?"** (Verma; arXiv 2026-08-12; ICML 2026 AI for Law workshop): legal citations. The model gets the passage and the cited document (only the pinpoint page when one is given) and answers yes or no with a rationale. Across 14 model configurations, models caught 93–100% of swapped cases but only 37–61% of wrong pinpoint pages in court opinions and 52–83% in briefs. When they missed, they tended to accept the citation because of topical overlap. Even GPT-5.4 with high reasoning effort missed 40% of pinpoint mismatches in opinions — [arXiv 2608.12571](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12571); [prismix summary](https://prismix.dev/news/25daadcf78ed).
- **Verifier strictness** (arXiv July 2026): on identical agent outputs, the unsupported-citation rate ranged from about 3% to about 18% depending only on the verifier's strictness. The authors propose an evaluation protocol anchored to gold labels and a deployable guard — [chatpaper summary](https://chatpaper.com/paper/313100); [arXiv 2607.20527](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20527).
- **Correctness versus faithfulness** (Wallat et al., arXiv December 2024): a citation can be correct (the document supports the statement) without being faithful (the model did not actually rely on it). Up to 57% of citations lacked faithfulness ("post-rationalization") in a Command-R+ setup on NaturalQuestions — [arXiv 2412.18004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.18004); [TU Delft](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/correctness-is-not-faithfulness-in-retrieval-augmented-generation/). A September 2026 reproduction found roughly one citation in seven unfaithful on Wikipedia-based questions, and RLVR did not fix it — [arXiv 2609.23053](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23053).

#### 2d. Attribution evaluation (does a source support a statement?)
- **ALCE** (Gao et al., EMNLP 2023): benchmarks LLMs that write text with citations (ASQA, QAMPARI, ELI5). Citation recall asks whether the cited passages together support the statement; citation precision asks whether each citation is needed. Both are scored with the TRUE NLI model (T5-11B). Even the best models lacked complete citation support 50% of the time on ELI5. Against human judgments, the automatic citation recall was 85.1% accurate and citation precision 77.6%. The NLI model cannot detect "partial support", which produces false positives for irrelevant citations — [arXiv 2305.14627](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14627); [review](https://liner.com/review/enabling-large-language-models-to-generate-text-with-citations). Code is MIT — [GitHub](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/ALCE).
- **AttrScore** (Yue et al., OSU, 2023): three labels. Attributable means the reference fully supports the claim; Extrapolatory means the reference lacks enough information; Contradictory means the claim contradicts the reference. Zero-shot GPT-4 scored 55.6% overall on the simulated test set (AttrEval-Simulation) and 85.1% on AttrEval-GenSearch, but only 23.2% and 45.0% on the Contradictory class. The best fine-tuned model on GenSearch (Flan-T5 3B) scored 75.2%. The authors warn that GPT-4 both generated and judged the GenSearch examples. MIT license; fine-tuned models on Hugging Face — [GitHub README](https://github.com/OSU-NLP-Group/AttrScore).
- **MiniCheck and LLM-AggreFact** (Tang, Laban, Durrett; EMNLP 2024): MiniCheck(document, sentence) returns a support probability between 0 and 1. Multi-sentence claims must first be split into sentences. LLM-AggreFact aggregates 11 grounded fact-checking datasets (labels: 1 supported, 0 not). Prefix caching cut checking of a 29K-example test set from 55 to 30 minutes on one A6000 GPU. Code is Apache 2.0 — [GitHub README](https://github.com/Liyan06/MiniCheck). Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B tops the leaderboard at 77.4% average balanced accuracy, against 71.8% for Vectara's HHEM-2.1 (vendor claim) — [Bespoke Labs](https://bespokelabs.ai/bespoke-minicheck). Its model license is listed as CC BY-NC 4.0, with commercial use by arrangement — [PromptLayer listing](https://www.promptlayer.com/models/bespoke-minicheck-7b); [GitHub README](https://github.com/Liyan06/MiniCheck). A 2025 paper cautions against relying on imperfect factuality metrics — [arXiv 2501.14883](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.14883).
- **RefChecker** (Amazon, arXiv 2405.14486, 2024): splits responses into knowledge triplets, checks each against reference text, and labels them factual, hallucinated or neutral. It covers three settings (no context, noisy context, accurate context) and comes with 2.1K human-annotated responses from seven LLMs. Apache 2.0 — [GitHub README](https://github.com/amazon-science/RefChecker).

#### 2e. Claim extraction
- **Claimify** (Metropolitansky & Larson, Microsoft Research; ACL 2025): extracts claims only when it is confident of the correct reading of ambiguous text. On BingCheck (Microsoft Copilot answers), against AFaCTA, Factcheck-GPT, VeriScore, DnD and SAFE: 99.0% of claims were entailed by the source (a statistical tie with VeriScore; DnD 89.1%). Element-level coverage was 87.9% against 76.9% for DnD. Decontextualization gave the desired outcome in 80.6% of cases against 78.4% for DnD — [ACL Anthology PDF](https://preview.aclanthology.org/landing_page/2025.acl-long.348.pdf); [Microsoft Research page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1130106); [Claimify blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1134179).
- **FActScore** (Min et al., EMNLP 2023): splits text into atomic facts and checks each against a knowledge source (default: a Wikipedia dump of 2023-04-01; custom sources such as the ACL Anthology are supported). The automatic estimator has under 2% error against human FActScores. Human-annotated scores for biographies: InstructGPT 42%, ChatGPT 58%, PerplexityAI 71%. ChatGPT fell from 80% to 16% for rarer people. Cost was about $1 of API per 100 sentences. MIT license — [GitHub README](https://github.com/shmsw25/FActScore); [paper summary](https://huggingface.co/papers/2305.14251); [Meta AI](https://ai.meta.com/research/publications/factscore-fine-grained-atomic-evaluation-of-factual-precision-in-long-form-text-generation).
- **VeriScore** (Song, Kim, Iyyer; Findings of EMNLP 2024): argues that FActScore and SAFE wrongly assume every claim is verifiable, so it extracts only verifiable claims, retrieves Google results through the Serper API, and verifies each. Labels are binary (supported, unsupported) or ternary (supported, contradicted, inconclusive). Fine-tuned extractor (Mistral) and verifier (Llama 3) models are released. Across 16 models, GPT-4o scored best, and a model's score on one task did not predict its score on another. Apache 2.0 — [GitHub README](https://github.com/Yixiao-Song/VeriScore); [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/fix-8040/2024.findings-emnlp.552); [review](https://themoonlight.io/review/veriscore-evaluating-the-factuality-of-verifiable-claims-in-long-form-text-generation).

#### 2f. Long-form factuality and open fact-checking frameworks
- **SAFE and LongFact** (Wei et al., Google DeepMind; arXiv 2403.18802, March 2024; NeurIPS 2024): an LLM splits a long response into atomic facts, writes Google Search queries for each, and judges whether the results support it. SAFE agreed with crowdsourced annotators on 72% of about 16,000 facts. On a random 100 of the disagreements, SAFE was right 76% of the time. It was more than 20 times cheaper than human annotators. LongFact has 2,280 prompts; the F1@K metric adds recall. Code Apache 2.0, other materials CC-BY — [GitHub README](https://github.com/google-deepmind/long-form-factuality); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802v2); [DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/research/publications/long-form-factuality-in-large-language-models/).
- **OpenFactCheck** (Wang et al., MBZUAI; arXiv 2405.05583, 2024): a framework with three modules: customizable fact-checker pipelines (including Factcheck-GPT, RARR and FacTool), automatic evaluation of LLM factuality with reports, and a leaderboard for comparing fact-checkers. MIT license — [GitHub README](https://github.com/yuxiaw/OpenFactCheck).
- **Loki / OpenFactVerification** (Li et al., Libr-AI; arXiv 2410.01794, 2024): a pipeline that splits text into claims, decides which are worth checking, generates search queries, collects web evidence (Serper) and verifies each claim. MIT license. Libr-AI also promotes an online service and a registered "Supporter Edition" with extra features — [GitHub README](https://github.com/Libr-AI/OpenFactVerification).
- **FacTool** (GAIR-NLP; arXiv 2307.13528, July 2023): a tool-augmented checker for knowledge QA, code, math and "scientific literature review". The last task detects hallucinated references (existence), not support. Its leaderboard rates chatbots, for example GPT-4 at 75.60 weighted claim-level accuracy and 43.33 response-level accuracy. Apache 2.0 — [GitHub README](https://github.com/GAIR-NLP/factool).

### Inferences
- **Base rates.** Medicine is the best-measured field, with about 17–25% of quotations wrong and 8–12% seriously wrong. A behavioral economics literature review with about 100 citing sentences may therefore contain several Major problems even if written by hand, and the AI-assisted case is likely worse given OpenScholar's 78–90% fabrication result. The skill should expect findings, not treat them as rare.
- **Map severity to the published error grades.** "Not supported, unrelated or contradicted" (Mogull's major errors) maps to Major. "Oversimplified or overgeneralized" maps to Minor or to "partly". This lets the skill follow a recognized taxonomy.
- **Agreement is low, so show the evidence.** Fine-tuned systems reach only 0.52 macro-F1, and humans agree only at κ 0.31 on accuracy. The skill should present quotes and reasoning and use the status "needs human judgment" freely. It should not issue bare verdicts.
- **A "partly" label is needed.** ALCE's NLI scorer cannot detect partial support, while WiCE and SemanticCite both include a partial class. WiCE's per-token "not supported" annotation is a good output format: it shows exactly which word, such as "a" in "studied a", lacks support.
- **Calibrate both failure directions.** Checkers accept citations that merely share a topic (Verma), reject correct ones (CiteGuard: 16–17% recall for a bare LLM judge), and shift with strictness (3–18% unsupported on identical outputs). The skill needs a small gold set and should record its verifier settings in every finding's trajectory.
- **Reuse by component:** Claimify's approach of extracting only when the reading is clear, and VeriScore's "verifiable claims only", for FACT claim extraction; the WiCE and SciFact citance-to-atomic-claim step for CIT-06; ALCE's citation recall and precision for sentences with several citations; MiniCheck or AttrScore models as cheap second opinions (checking licenses: Bespoke-7B is non-commercial); SAFE, VeriScore or Loki query generation for FACT-01 web evidence.
- **Faithfulness of the checker itself.** Wallat et al. show that a model can attach a passage after answering from memory. The skill's own verdicts must therefore rest on text actually retrieved from the cited source, quoted verbatim and checked by a script, never on what the model remembers about a famous paper.

- AutoAIS (attribution scored with a T5 NLI model, from the AIS framework and Attributed QA work) was not researched separately. ALCE's TRUE-NLI scorer is the closest sourced example, and its "no partial support" limitation applies.
- Citation text generation datasets (for example CiteBench) and cite-worthiness datasets for CIT-07 (for example CiteWorth or Wikipedia "citation needed") were not researched; the search budget ran out.

## 3. Retrieval depth, absence of evidence, verdict categories, reported accuracies, failure modes

### Takeaway
Abstracts are enough to confirm some citations but not to reject one. Citing sentences often refer to things that appear only in the body of the cited paper. A verdict that "the source never mentions a" therefore requires the full text, searched with an expanded list of terms. With only the abstract, the honest verdict is "cannot verify". Verdict schemes in the literature consistently separate "contradicted" from "not enough information", and the better ones add "partially supported".

### Cited Findings

#### Retrieval depth: abstract or full text
- SciFact checks claims against abstracts only, and annotators found evidence in the abstracts of only some of the cited documents (two of three in the documented example) — [SciFact data docs](https://github.com/allenai/scifact/blob/master/doc/data.md).
- Citing sentences cover most of what the abstract says, plus about 20% more concepts not in the abstract (Divoli, Nakov & Hearst 2012, molecular interactions) — [PMC3514807](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3514807/). Citation summaries overlap with abstracts but stress different aspects (Elkiss et al. 2008) — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jamist/v59y2008i1p51-62.html).
- LitQA2 was designed so that answers require the main text rather than the abstract — [arXiv 2409.13740](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13740v2).
- Full-text systems: SemanticCite analyzes "complete source documents" — [GitHub](https://github.com/sebhaan/semanticcite); RefVerifier locates evidence in the full text — [arXiv 2609.07652](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07652); sciwrite-lint downloads and parses cited papers — [arXiv 2604.08501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08501); PaperQA2 indexes full-text PDFs — [GitHub](https://github.com/Future-House/paper-qa).
- Counterpoint: in fine-tuned quotation-error detection, adding the cited work's abstract beat two other ways of adding full-text information — [arXiv 2606.08589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08589). GPT models detected erroneous citations "with limited context" — [arXiv 2411.06101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06101).
- Long cited documents need evidence retrieval before an entailment model can judge them (WiCE); an "oracle retrieval" version shows the upper bound — [WiCE README](https://github.com/ryokamoi/wice).
- Location matters: models caught swapped cases (93–100%) far more often than wrong pinpoint pages (37–61% in opinions) — [arXiv 2608.12571](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12571).

#### Absence of evidence
- AttrScore's "Extrapolatory" label is for references that lack enough information to validate the claim — [AttrScore README](https://github.com/OSU-NLP-Group/AttrScore). VeriScore's ternary scheme has "inconclusive" — [VeriScore README](https://github.com/Yixiao-Song/VeriScore). SemanticCite's "Unsupported" covers a source that lacks the claim, and "Uncertain" covers ambiguous evidence — [review summary](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/semanticcite-citation-verification-with-ai-powered-full-text-analysis-and-evidence-based-reasoning). The citation-integrity corpus has an "irrelevant" label — [PMC11231046](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11231046/).
- PaperQA2 abstains with "insufficient information" on about 21.9% of LitQA2 questions, which is why its precision (85.2%) is much higher than its accuracy (66.0%) — [arXiv 2409.13740](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13740v2).
- Mogull's major-error definition includes a cited source that is unrelated to the claim — [Texas State repository](https://digital.library.txstate.edu/handle/10877/8673).

#### Failure modes documented
- Accepting a citation because it shares the topic, even when the specific point is absent — [arXiv 2608.12571](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12571).
- Rejecting correct citations: a bare LLM judge had 16–17% recall — [CiteGuard](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.acl-long.282/).
- Results that depend on verifier strictness (about 3% to 18% unsupported on identical outputs) — [arXiv 2607.20527](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20527).
- NLI scorers that cannot see partial support, producing false positives — [ALCE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14627).
- Weak detection of the "contradictory" class (23–45% for zero-shot GPT-4) — [AttrScore README](https://github.com/OSU-NLP-Group/AttrScore).
- Better at confirming accurate citations than at catching wrong ones (GPT-4: 0.65 micro-F1 vs 0.45 macro-F1) — [PMC11231046](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11231046/).
- Supporting quotes that do not reproduce across runs (46% match in Elicit) — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/C97DAEC70C3173A260F0B12E729E7250/core-reader).
- Post-rationalized citations: up to 57% unfaithful in one setup — [arXiv 2412.18004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.18004).
- Collapsing to the majority label: scite labeled most supporting or contrasting citations as "mentioning" — [Hypothesis 2023](https://journals.indianapolis.iu.edu/index.php/hypothesis/article/view/26528).
- Large drops when moving from a small closed corpus to an open one (at least 15 F1 on SciFact-Open) — [arXiv 2210.13777](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.13777).
- Lower factual precision for rare entities (FActScore: 80% to 16%) — [paper summary](https://huggingface.co/papers/2305.14251).

### Inferences
- **Rule for CIT-06.** "Does not support (not mentioned)" may be issued only after the full text was searched with the expanded term list. With only the abstract, say "cannot verify: not in the abstract, full text needed", unless the abstract itself clearly supports or contradicts the claim. This turns DESIGN.md's "Paywalled sources" limit into a firm rule. It rests on Divoli et al.'s 20% of extra concepts and on LitQA2's design.
- **Proof of absence** should list every term searched, the hit count per term, the sections and pages covered, the file version used, and any parse warnings. This mirrors the guardrail "Not found is not fake".
- **Proposed labels** for CIT-06 and FACT-02: Supports; Partly supports (with what is missing or overgeneralized); Does not support: topic absent; Does not support: mentions the topic but says something else (misattribution); Contradicts; Cannot verify (no full text, parse failure, or ambiguous). This keeps DESIGN.md's four labels while splitting "does not" into the cases that need different fixes.
- **Topical-overlap guard.** For "prior work studied a" claims, the judge must name the specific concept a and quote a passage containing it (or an approved synonym). Overlap with the paper's general topic is explicitly not enough.

### Gaps
- No study measured how often supporting evidence for citations in economics papers is found only in the body rather than the abstract. The roughly 20% extra-concept figure comes from molecular biology.
- The Huang et al. (2026) result that the abstract worked best may reflect noise or context limits of fine-tuned small models. Their full text could not be read to check.
- FEVER's "NOT ENOUGH INFO" definitions and SAFE's exact label set were not re-verified in this session.

## 4. How should synonyms and paraphrase be handled (for example "present bias" vs "hyperbolic discounting")?

### Takeaway
Use a hybrid approach: lexical search for exact terms, which is strong on scientific text, plus dense retrieval and LLM-generated paraphrases for wording differences, plus an economics thesaurus that records how terms relate. The relation matters for the verdict. "Present bias" and "hyperbolic discounting" are related but not identical: the β-δ (quasi-hyperbolic) model captures present bias as one feature of hyperbolic discounting. A citation that says "studied present bias" but points to a paper that only fits a hyperbolic discount function deserves "partly supports" with an explanation, not "supports".

### Cited Findings
- On the SciFact retrieval task in BEIR, BM25 is very strong zero-shot (Recall@100 0.908), ahead of DPR (0.727), ANCE (0.816), TAS-B (0.891) and GenQ (0.893). This is the Recall@100 table as summarized in search results; some entries carry footnotes, and the nDCG@10 figures were not verified — [BEIR, arXiv 2104.08663](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.08663). Dense models trained on MS MARCO tend to do worse out of domain — [Elastic blog](https://www.elastic.co/search-labs/blog/improving-information-retrieval-elastic-stack-benchmarking-passage-retrieval).
- SemanticCite combines BM25 with dense vectors and reranks with FlashRank, after an LLM "refiner" extracts the core claim from the citing text — [GitHub README](https://github.com/sebhaan/semanticcite). PaperQA2 combines keyword search to narrow the chunks with embeddings and LLM re-scoring; its agent can iteratively refine queries — [GitHub README](https://github.com/Future-House/paper-qa). ReCite uses "intent-aware query planning" — [arXiv 2609.09156](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09156).
- STW Thesaurus for Economics (ZBW): a subject-category taxonomy, thousands of descriptors, and tens of thousands of synonyms and links between concepts, in SKOS (prefLabel, altLabel for synonyms, broader, narrower, related). It maps to JEL, Wikidata, DBpedia, EuroVoc and others. Version 9.10 (2020-10-22) is licensed ODbL 1.0. Deprecated concepts keep resolvable identifiers — [ZBW STW 9.10](https://www.zbw.eu/stw/version/9.10/download/about.en.html); [W3C case study](https://www.w3.org/2001/sw/sweo/public/UseCases/ZBW).
- Present bias and hyperbolic discounting: quasi-hyperbolic discounting simplifies hyperbolic discounting and captures one of its main features, present bias — [Duke lecture notes](https://sites.duke.edu/toddsarver/files/2024/01/5-Present-Biased-Preferences.pdf). In the β-δ model, β is the present-bias parameter and δ the long-run exponential discount factor. A meta-analysis estimates β at 0.938 for money and 0.750 for non-monetary rewards — [Cheung, Tymula & Wang, Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2023.04003); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=5222564). Bleichrodt et al. propose an alternative index τ = ln β / ln δ — [Bleichrodt et al.](https://personal.eur.nl/bleichrodt/Taudelta6Apr2017.pdf).
- Citing sentences use concepts beyond those in the abstract (about 20% more), so other papers' descriptions of a work are a source of alternative wording — [PMC3514807](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3514807/). Semantic Scholar exposes these citing sentences as `contexts` — [S2 notebook](https://media.sciencemediacenter.de/share/mewikoco-ds/12_s2_reference_details.html).
- WiCE annotates the specific tokens in a claim that the source does not support — [WiCE README](https://github.com/ryokamoi/wice).

### Inferences
- **Build a term set for each attributed concept**: (1) the exact phrase and its morphological variants; (2) synonyms from the STW thesaurus (altLabel) plus broader, narrower and related concepts, each tagged with its relation; (3) LLM-generated paraphrases and the formal name of the model, for example "β-δ", "quasi-hyperbolic", "(β, δ) preferences" or "time-inconsistent preferences", which reviewers can extend in a project glossary; (4) phrases taken from Semantic Scholar citation contexts of the cited paper.
- **Search the full text twice**: an exhaustive lexical scan for every term (cheap and fully reproducible, and it gives the absence proof), then hybrid dense-plus-BM25 retrieval with reranking to catch paraphrases no list anticipated.
- **Verdict depends on the relation**: an exact or synonym match can support; a broader, narrower or related match gives at most "partly", and the finding names the relation ("the source studies hyperbolic discounting functions; it does not identify present bias as such"); a match only on the general topic is never support, per Verma (2026).
- **Equations and parameter names count as evidence** in economics: a source that estimates β < 1 in a β-δ model supports "present bias" even if it never uses the phrase. The judge prompt should say so, and the parser must keep math. PaperQA2's December 2025 readers and GROBID handle equations and structure to differing degrees.

### Gaps
- No evaluation was found of synonym-aware citation verification in economics, nor of whether STW coverage is current for behavioral terms. The latest STW version confirmed here is 9.10 from 2020.
- The JEL classification was not checked as a synonym source; it is coarse (field codes, not concepts) and probably useful only for routing.

## 5. How can full text of paywalled economics papers be obtained legitimately?

### Takeaway
Use a cascade of legal sources and cache the result: first the user's own PDFs (a folder of papers they can access through their library); then open-access copies found through OpenAlex, Unpaywall and Semantic Scholar; then working-paper versions (NBER papers become open after 18 months) and CORE. Fall back to the abstract and label the verdict accordingly. Always record which version was read, because a submitted or working-paper version "may differ significantly" from the published article.

### Cited Findings
- **OpenAlex**: each work has `primary_location`, `best_oa_location` and a `locations` list. Each location has `is_oa`, `pdf_url`, `landing_page_url`, `license` and `version` (publishedVersion, acceptedVersion, submittedVersion). OpenAlex warns that the submitted version "may differ significantly" from the published one, and it defines open access broadly as readable full text without paying or logging in — [OpenAlex location object docs](https://docs.openalex.org/api-entities/works/work-object/location-object). Its content API serves cached PDFs at `content.openalex.org/works/{id}.pdf` for $0.01 per file, with a free key covering about 100 files per day. A `has_content.pdf` filter finds works with a cached PDF; coverage is given as about 50 million or about 60 million works on different pages — [OpenAlex full-text PDFs](https://developers.openalex.org/download/full-text-pdfs); [OpenAlex help](https://help.openalex.org/access/fulltext.md).
- **Unpaywall**: `https://api.unpaywall.org/v2/{DOI}?email=…` with a required email. `best_oa_location` prefers published versions over preprints and gives `url_for_pdf`, `host_type`, `version` and `license`. `oa_status` is gold, hybrid, bronze, green or closed. About 100,000 calls per day per user are suggested; for more, use the data dump — [rOpenSci roadoi docs](https://docs.ropensci.org/roadoi/reference/oadoi_fetch.html); [roadoi manual](https://packages.ropensci.org/roadoi/doc/manual.html); [third-party guide](https://playbooks.com/skills/kthorn/research-superpower/finding-open-access-papers). PaperQA2 uses Unpaywall for open-access links — [PaperQA2 README](https://github.com/Future-House/paper-qa).
- **Semantic Scholar**: `openAccessPdf` returns {url, status, license} — [Apify docs](https://apify.com/crawlerbros/semanticscholar-scraper). Snippet search returns passages from paper text — [ToolUniverse](https://zitniklab.hms.harvard.edu/ToolUniverse/tools/semantic_scholar_tools.html).
- **CORE**: the v3 API (`api.core.ac.uk/v3/search/works`) requires a key. Third-party figures put CORE at about 300 million records with over 40 million full texts from more than 10,000 repositories; another source says 130 million articles — [Archivarix](https://echo.archivarix.net/archive/core); [CORE API guide](https://www.skills.sh/wentorai/research-plugins/core-api-guide); [CORE blog](https://blog.core.ac.uk/category/harvesting).
- **NBER**: working papers issued more than 18 months ago are open access. Newer ones are free to subscribing institutions, NBER Corporate Associates, US government employees, journalists and residents of low-income countries; everyone else gets 3 free papers a year — [NBER Working Papers](https://www.nber.org/papers).
- **Parsing**: GROBID turns PDFs into structured TEI XML (header, sections, references, citation callouts, figures, tables). Reference parsing scores about 0.87–0.90 F1, citation-context resolution 0.76–0.91 F1 depending on the test set, and DOI/PMID matching above 0.95 F1. Deep-learning models beat the default CRF models. Apache 2.0. A companion tool builds full-text corpora from a list of DOIs by finding open-access PDFs — [GROBID README](https://github.com/kermitt2/grobid).
- **User-supplied PDFs** are supported by PaperQA2 (a local paper directory), SemanticCite (upload) and sciwrite-lint (runs locally) — [PaperQA2](https://github.com/Future-House/paper-qa); [SemanticCite](https://github.com/sebhaan/semanticcite); [sciwrite-lint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08501).
- RefVerifier's authors note that wider deployment depends on rights-cleared full-text access through institutional partnerships — [arXiv 2609.07652](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07652).

### Inferences
- **Proposed cascade per reference**, cached in `paper-check-work/fulltext/`:
  1. the user's folder of PDFs, matched by DOI or fuzzy title;
  2. OpenAlex `best_oa_location.pdf_url`, then the other `locations` with a `pdf_url`, preferring publishedVersion or acceptedVersion;
  3. Unpaywall `best_oa_location.url_for_pdf`;
  4. Semantic Scholar `openAccessPdf`;
  5. working-paper copies (NBER if older than 18 months, or other copies OpenAlex lists as repository locations), marked as "submittedVersion";
  6. CORE;
  7. OpenAlex content API ($0.01 per file) if the user enables it;
  8. abstract only, from OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar or Crossref.
  When nothing is found, the report should list the papers whose PDFs the user should add.
- **Record the version in every proof** (published, accepted, or working paper and date). If only a working paper is available, the verdict carries the caveat "checked against the working paper version". This matters for FACT-03, where copied numbers may differ between versions.
- Shadow libraries are excluded. The skill should say so in its coverage section so users know paywalled items were judged from abstracts.
- **Network**: DESIGN.md already notes that this cloud session blocks these hosts. The cascade must handle a lookup being unavailable and report it as "skipped", not "passed".

### Gaps
- Access rules for SSRN, RePEc/IDEAS, EconStor, IZA discussion papers, CEPR and authors' homepages could not be verified because the search budget ran out. They are likely important working-paper sources in economics and should be checked before implementation.
- No figures were found for the share of economics or behavioral economics articles with a legal open-access copy.
- Semantic Scholar's official definitions of `openAccessPdf.status` and its usage terms were not retrieved.
- Publisher text-and-data-mining APIs (for example Elsevier or Wiley) were not researched.

## 6. Proposed evidence-backed pipeline: from citing sentence to verdict with quoted proof

### Takeaway
Treat each citing sentence as one or more atomic "attributed propositions". For each cited work: get the full text legally, search it exhaustively with an expanded term set, retrieve the best passages with hybrid search, and have Claude judge with a fine-grained label and verbatim quotes. A script then confirms each quote is in the source, and an independent agent tries to overturn the verdict. Calibrate on a small gold set that includes the coauthor's real errors. Each step is backed by a published system, but expect only about 70% agreement with careful humans at first, so the report should present the evidence and let people decide.

### Cited Findings (evidence behind each step)
- **Atomic claims from citing sentences**: SciFact rewrote citances into atomic claims — [SciFact README](https://github.com/allenai/scifact). WiCE's Claim-Split and per-token support annotation — [WiCE](https://github.com/ryokamoi/wice). Claimify extracts only when the reading is clear (99.0% entailment) — [ACL 2025](https://preview.aclanthology.org/landing_page/2025.acl-long.348.pdf). MiniCheck needs single-sentence claims — [MiniCheck](https://github.com/Liyan06/MiniCheck).
- **Several citations in one sentence**: SciFact's `cited_doc_ids`, where evidence was found in only some of the cited documents — [SciFact data docs](https://github.com/allenai/scifact/blob/master/doc/data.md). ALCE's citation recall (the citations together support the statement) and citation precision (each citation is needed) — [ALCE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14627).
- **Mimic the human reviewer: context, then retrieval, then judgment**: the PRC 2025 three-step design — [PRC](https://peerreviewcongress.org/abstract/leveraging-large-language-models-for-detecting-citation-quotation-errors-in-biomedical-literature/); retriever plus verifier in the citation-integrity corpus — [PMC11231046](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11231046/); SemanticCite's five stages — [SemanticCite](https://github.com/sebhaan/semanticcite).
- **Full text, page anchors, and parsing**: PaperQA2's page-numbered readers — [PaperQA2](https://github.com/Future-House/paper-qa); GROBID TEI and citation callouts — [GROBID](https://github.com/kermitt2/grobid); pinpoint-page errors are missed often — [arXiv 2608.12571](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12571).
- **Hybrid retrieval and reranking**: BEIR (BM25 strong on SciFact) — [BEIR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.08663); SemanticCite (BM25, dense, FlashRank) — [SemanticCite](https://github.com/sebhaan/semanticcite); PaperQA2's RCS — [PaperQA2](https://github.com/Future-House/paper-qa).
- **Fine-grained labels and abstention**: WiCE, AttrScore, SemanticCite, VeriScore (see the table in section 3); PaperQA2 abstains about 21.9% of the time — [arXiv 2409.13740](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13740v2).
- **Second-opinion checkers**: MiniCheck and Bespoke-MiniCheck (77.4% balanced accuracy, non-commercial license) — [Bespoke](https://bespokelabs.ai/bespoke-minicheck); AttrScore Flan-T5 models (MIT) — [AttrScore](https://github.com/OSU-NLP-Group/AttrScore); SemanticCite Checker-Qwen3-4B — [SemanticCite](https://github.com/sebhaan/semanticcite).
- **Search when the judge says "no"**: CiteGuard's retrieval-augmented validation raised CiteME accuracy to 68.1%, while a bare LLM judge rejected correct citations (16–17% recall) — [CiteGuard](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.acl-long.282/).
- **Calibrate the verifier**: unsupported rates of 3–18% from strictness alone; protocol anchored to gold labels — [arXiv 2607.20527](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20527). Gold sets available: the citation-integrity corpus (3,063 citations) — [PMC11231046](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11231046/); SemanticCite's dataset (1,000+ citations, 8 disciplines) — [arXiv 2511.16198](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.16198); WiCE — [WiCE](https://github.com/ryokamoi/wice); SciFact — [SciFact](https://github.com/allenai/scifact); LLM-AggreFact — [MiniCheck](https://github.com/Liyan06/MiniCheck).
- **FACT checks** (web and scholarly evidence): SAFE, VeriScore and Loki generate search queries per claim and verify against retrieved evidence — [SAFE](https://github.com/google-deepmind/long-form-factuality); [VeriScore](https://github.com/Yixiao-Song/VeriScore); [Loki](https://github.com/Libr-AI/OpenFactVerification). VeriScore's ternary labels — [VeriScore](https://github.com/Yixiao-Song/VeriScore). FActScore records the knowledge-source date (a 2023-04-01 Wikipedia dump) — [FActScore](https://github.com/shmsw25/FActScore).
- **Priority and related-work searches (FACT-06, CIT-09)**: without tools, LLMs find the cited paper only 4.2–18.5% of the time, against 35.3% for a search agent, 68.1% for CiteGuard and 69.7% for humans — [CiteME](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/0ef47f7b768e1a012e3d995ac8d8fac7-Abstract.html); [CiteGuard](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.acl-long.282/). OpenScholar and PaperQA2 do retrieval-grounded synthesis — [OpenScholar](https://github.com/AkariAsai/OpenScholar); [PaperQA2](https://github.com/Future-House/paper-qa).
- **Cost and time references**: SemanticCite takes 5–15 seconds per citation (cloud) — [SemanticCite](https://github.com/sebhaan/semanticcite); RefVerifier a median of 11 minutes per manuscript — [arXiv 2609.07652](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07652); FActScore about $1 per 100 sentences (2023 API prices) — [FActScore](https://github.com/shmsw25/FActScore); SAFE more than 20 times cheaper than human annotators — [SAFE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802v2).

### Inferences

#### Pipeline for CIT-06, CIT-08, FACT-02 and CIT-13 (one subagent per batch of references; each step's output is cached and goes into the trajectory)
1. **Find citing units** (SCRIPT). From the paper map, take each sentence with an in-text citation, plus narrative attributions without a bracketed citation ("Laibson (1997) shows…"), which also covers FACT-02. Keep the anchor (`file:line` or page and line) and one sentence of context before and after.
2. **Split into attributed propositions** (READ, Claimify and WiCE style). Break the sentence into atomic propositions and attach each to the reference or references it cites. Tag the attribution type: studied or used topic X; found or reported result Y; proposed method or model Z; defined term T; reported number N (which also triggers FACT-03); or general background. Mark propositions too vague to check as "not check-worthy" (VeriScore, Loki). Mark which words carry the claim (for example "a" in "studied a").
3. **Route by depth**. In standard mode, check "found", "number" and "studied X" propositions; in deep mode, check everything. Background citations of the "see X for a review" kind get a light check that the topic appears in the abstract. Citation intent (SciCite labels from Semantic Scholar) can help decide this.
4. **Resolve the reference** (LOOKUP, already CIT-02 and CIT-03). Get the DOI and OpenAlex/S2 IDs and record the version.
5. **Get the full text** (LOOKUP). Use the cascade in section 5, then parse with GROBID or PyMuPDF keeping page numbers, section headings and equations. Record the source URL, version and parse quality. If only the abstract is available, flag the proposition as "abstract-only".
6. **Expand terms** (READ plus SCRIPT). For each key concept, use STW synonyms and relations, LLM paraphrases and model names (for example β-δ), the project glossary, and phrases from Semantic Scholar citation contexts. Store the term list in the trajectory.
7. **Exhaustive scan** (SCRIPT). Count hits for every term across the full text by section and page, and include the abstract and appendix. This produces the absence proof or the candidate passages.
8. **Retrieve passages** (SCRIPT). Hybrid BM25 plus dense retrieval over chunks, reranked, plus the passages found in step 7, each with one neighbouring chunk for context. Keep the top 5–15, following PaperQA2's `evidence_k` settings.
9. **Judge** (READ, Claude). Input: the proposition, the citing sentence with context, the passages with page numbers, the term relations and the version. Output one label: Supports; Partly supports (oversimplified, overgeneralized, wrong scope or population, a related but not identical concept); Does not support: topic absent; Does not support: misattributed (the source discusses the topic but says something else); Contradicts; Cannot verify. Also output verbatim evidence quotes with page numbers, the claim words not supported (as in WiCE), and a confidence level. Rules: overlap with the general topic is not support; "Topic absent" requires the step 7 scan of the full text; abstract-only sources may receive only Supports, Contradicts or Cannot verify. Several citations in one sentence are scored ALCE-style: is the sentence supported by all its citations together, and does each citation contribute?
10. **Verify quotes** (SCRIPT, extending `verify_quotes.py`). Every evidence quote must match the cached source text after normalizing whitespace and hyphenation. Failing quotes are repaired or the finding is dropped. For CIT-13, compare the draft's direct quotations with the source word for word and check the page number against the parsed page anchors.
11. **Refutation pass** (READ, a separate agent; DESIGN.md guardrail 2). For "does not support" and "contradicts" verdicts, search harder (more paraphrases, appendix, tables, footnotes) to look for support, since bare LLM judges reject correct citations. For "supports" verdicts on important claims, try to show the passage is only about the general topic. Optionally, ensemble with a second checker (MiniCheck or AttrScore, minding licenses). Disagreement leads to "needs human judgment".
12. **Report**. Severity follows the published grades: Contradicts, topic absent and misattributed are Major (the "major error" definition); Partly is Minor or Major depending on how central the claim is; Cannot verify is reported with a fix of type "investigate" and a request for the PDF. Every finding includes the citing sentence quote and anchor, the evidence quotes with pages, or the absence proof (terms, hit counts, sections, version); a suggested fix (rewrite the sentence to match the source, or find a better citation through a CiteME/CiteGuard-style search); and the trajectory.

#### FACT-01 to FACT-06
- **FACT-01 (external facts)**: extract Claimify-style, verifiable-only claims; for each, generate 2–3 search queries; retrieve web or scholarly evidence; judge with Verified, Contradicted, Outdated (the evidence is dated after the claim or reports a change) or Unverifiable, in line with VeriScore's ternary labels plus a time dimension. Record the date of each evidence item, as FActScore records its Wikipedia dump date.
- **FACT-02 and FACT-03**: run the CIT-06 pipeline on narrative attributions. For copied baseline numbers, retrieve the source table (PaperQA2's 2025 readers parse tables), compare the numbers and the setting, and record the version, since working papers can differ.
- **FACT-04 (names)**: check names against the resolved metadata (authors, methods, datasets) from step 4. No dedicated research was found.
- **FACT-05 (stale statements)**: date-aware search; any evidence newer than the draft's claim about "recent" or "latest" work becomes a Minor finding.
- **FACT-06 (priority claims) and CIT-09 (missing related work)**: agentic search with many queries (OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar search and snippets, citation graph of the closest papers). Report the queries and the closest hits. Given CiteME, no hit is not proof of novelty, so the verdict is "no contrary evidence found in N searches", never "verified novel".

#### Calibration before trusting verdicts
- Build a gold set of 50–100 attributed propositions from real behavioral economics drafts. Include the coauthor's errors and about one-third correct citations, and have two people label it. Measure agreement with Claude's verdicts by label, and tune strictness against both failure directions (topical-overlap false accepts, false rejects). Run SciFact, the citation-integrity corpus or SemanticCite's dataset as regression tests when prompts change. Report the verifier configuration in the coverage section.

#### Rough cost
- About 5–15 seconds of model time per citation (SemanticCite), or about 11 minutes per manuscript (RefVerifier). A 100-citation literature review should take tens of minutes in deep mode with parallel subagents. PDF retrieval and parsing, not the model, will usually be the bottleneck.

### Gaps
- No published system targets economics or behavioral economics citations, so accuracy for this domain is unknown until the gold set exists.
- Whether Claude-class models in 2026 make the misattribution errors seen in older studies at the same rates is untested here. The newest legal results (GPT-5.4 still missing 40% of pinpoint errors) suggest the problem persists.
- Licenses and maintenance status of the 2026 research tools (sciwrite-lint, RefVerifier, CiteAudit, ReCite, CiteGuard) were not checked. Treat them as design references until their repositories are reviewed.
